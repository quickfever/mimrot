Unveiling good and bad behaviors on the Agentic Internet

Jin-Hee Lee and Marina Elmore
10 MINUTE READ

The Internet isn't a single lane of traffic. For a long time, the rule of thumb in web security was that bots are bad, while humans are good. Of course, we're far past this generalization. Humans can be fraudulent, and bots can be helpful at different levels. Site owners actively want some automated traffic to interact with our sites to make the Internet functional and discoverable.

Defining Risk and Trust

When evaluating agentic systems, risk isn't static. An agent performing data collection for search indexing presents a drastically lower risk profile than an autonomous agent attempting high-frequency credential stuffing. Trust must be earned dynamically through cryptographic signatures and verified behavioral patterns.

Good behaviors, rooted in transparency

Well-behaved agents declare their identity clearly in user-agent strings and abide by robots.txt, rate limits, and site preferences. They operate predictably and respect server bandwidth constraints.

"Transparency is the cornerstone of the agentic web. If an agent disguises its intent, it forfeits trust."

Bad behaviors: blatant, stealthy, and everything in between

On the opposite end, malicious bots attempt fingerprint spoofing, IP rotation across residential proxy networks, and aggressive content scraping without attribution or consent.

Adaptive Intelligence is coming soon

Next-generation edge security models use machine learning inference at the edge to continuously update threat scores without adding latency to legitimate human and agent traffic.

Moving beyond determinism to influence bot behavior

Instead of binary blocking, modern web platforms employ dynamic challenge suites, rate limiting, and agentic negotiations to guide traffic safely.

Establish the Trust ecosystem that works for you

By adopting granular security rules and automated agent management, site owners can capture the benefits of AI automation while safeguarding infrastructure.
